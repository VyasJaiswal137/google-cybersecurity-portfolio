## 📋 1. Frameworks, Controls & Compliance Basics

* **CIA Triad:** A foundational model (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability) that helps organizations measure risk when setting up systems and security policies.
* **Security Frameworks:** Step-by-step guidelines used to build plans that protect data and privacy.
* **Security Controls:** Specific safeguards designed to reduce targeted security risks.
* **Compliance:** Following internal company standards and official external laws.

### 4 Core Components of a Security Framework
1. **Identify and Document Goals:** Define what security targets need to be reached.
2. **Set Guidelines:** Create clear rules to meet those security targets.
3. **Implement Security Processes:** Put actual security steps into practice.
4. **Monitor and Communicate:** Track results continuously and report findings.

## 📜 2. Common Frameworks & Regulations

| Framework / Regulation | What It Is | Key Purpose & Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **NIST (CSF & RMF)** | US National Institute of Standards and Technology | Voluntary guidelines used worldwide to manage and reduce risk. |
| **FERC-NERC** | Power Grid Regulations | Mandatory rules for electricity companies to protect power grid infrastructure. Must follow Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) reliability standards. |
| **FedRAMP** | US Cloud Security Program | Standardizes safety checks for cloud service providers working with the US government. |
| **CIS** | Center for Internet Security | Non-profit offering actionable controls to defend systems against cyberattacks. |
| **GDPR** | General Data Protection Regulation | European law protecting resident data privacy. Requires notifying affected individuals within **72 hours** of a breach. |
| **PCI DSS** | Payment Card Standard | Global rules for any company handling credit card data to prevent credit card fraud. |
| **HIPAA** | Patient Health Law | US federal law protecting private patient health data. Governed by 3 rules: **Privacy**, **Security**, and **Breach Notification**. |
| **ISO** | International Standards Body | Creates global standards across technology, manufacturing, and business management. |
| **SOC 1 & SOC 2** | AICPA Audit Reports | Reports evaluating user access permissions, financial rules, and data security risks across company levels. |

## ⚔️ 3. Ethics & Cyber Counterattacks

A **counterattack** means hacking back against a threat actor who attacked you.

### A. US Standpoint
* Counterattacking is **illegal** under laws like the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act.
* Hacking back is seen as **vigilantism** (taking the law into your own hands).
* Counterattacks can **escalate the damage** or trigger major international issues if the hacker is backed by a government.
* **Only approved federal government or military teams** are legally allowed to counterattack in the US.

### B. International Standpoint (ICJ Guidelines)
The **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** allows a counterattack *only* if it meets 4 strict rules:
1. It affects **only** the original attacker.
2. It acts as direct communication telling the attacker to stop.
3. It does **not** make the situation worse (no escalation).
4. The damage or effects can be completely reversed.

> *Note: Because these 4 conditions are very hard to guarantee, most companies never attempt counterattacks.*

## 🔐 4. Data Privacy & Ethical Principles

* **Confidentiality:** Making sure data is accessible **only** to authorized users.
* **Privacy Protection:** Keeping personal information safe from unauthorized access.
* **Laws:** Recognized rules enforced by a governing authority.

### PII vs. SPII Data

* **PII (Personally Identifiable Information):** Any basic information that can be used to identify an individual.
  * *Examples:* Full name, phone number, home address.
* **SPII (Sensitive Personally Identifiable Information):** High-risk personal details requiring stricter protection because exposure causes severe harm.
  * *Examples:* Social Security numbers (SSN), credit card numbers, medical records.
